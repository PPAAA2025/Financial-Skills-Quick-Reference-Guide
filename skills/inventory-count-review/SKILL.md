# Inventory Count Review

## Workflow

1. Confirm count date, warehouses, units of measure, item keys, ownership/consignment treatment, and the user's approved costing method.
2. Preserve original files. Normalize item codes and units in a documented working copy; do not merge different units or lots without an approved rule.
3. Compare physical quantities to book quantities. Reconcile count cut-off using supplied receipts, dispatches, transfers, and returns around the count date.
4. List shortages, surpluses, negative quantities, duplicate count lines, uncounted items, and records without item/location keys with source references.
5. Calculate days since last movement and flag slow/no-movement items using a threshold provided by the user. If none is given, ask before labeling inventory as obsolete.
6. For valuation concerns, list the evidence required (recent selling prices, completion/selling costs, damage, expiry, demand) but do not conclude a write-down without that evidence and the applicable accounting policy.

## Deliverables

- Count-to-book reconciliation by item and location.
- Exception and recount list with quantities and source rows.
- Slow-movement indicators and valuation evidence gaps.
- Assumptions, cut-off limitations, and unresolved items.

Require human approval before stock adjustments or write-down entries.
